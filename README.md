# Intelligence Assessment - Healthcare SaaS Supply Chain Risk

This repository collects a structured intelligence assessment on the systemic cyber risk associated with healthcare SaaS concentration and supply-chain compromise scenarios.

## Objective

The objective is not exploitation or offensive tradecraft. The work applies intelligence methodology to resilience, dependency concentration, disruption scenarios, and defensive implications for critical healthcare operations.

## Key Judgments

1. Healthcare SaaS centralization creates systemic amplification risk.
2. A compromise in a widely adopted provider could generate cascading operational disruption.
3. Identity, segmentation, and third-party resilience maturity remain uneven across the sector.
4. Double-extortion models increase reputational and policy pressure beyond the initial technical impact.

## Repository Contents

The assessment is structured around:
- executive framing and scope
- key judgments and confidence statements
- threat landscape context
- attack scenario modeling
- MITRE ATT&CK mapping
- technical tradecraft analysis
- systemic risk assessment
- detection opportunities
- strategic recommendations
- limitations and alternative hypotheses

## Analytical Method

The repository follows an intelligence-style approach:
- explicit assumptions
- confidence levels
- alternative hypotheses
- stated limitations
- strategic and operational implications

All scenarios are modeled from publicly documented ransomware and supply-chain tradecraft patterns observed between 2023 and 2025.

## Intended Audience

- cybersecurity learners
- analysts and defenders
- risk, resilience, and governance stakeholders
- anyone interested in how cyber incidents can scale through shared SaaS dependencies

## Scope Note

This is a defensive and analytical case-study repository. It is designed to reason about risk, impact, and preparedness, not to provide offensive guidance.
